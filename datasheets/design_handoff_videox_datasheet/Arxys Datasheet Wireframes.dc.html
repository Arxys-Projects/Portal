Two-page datasheet — three structural directions

Content pacing is real — the recommended-usage paragraph, five key attributes, five feature blocks, the camera-count matrix, the three spec sections, and the compliance strip are all present at true length so the density is judgeable. Same three directions, the simpler layout. That inheritance is the actual test of whether a direction is a system or just a nice page.

On the Management sheet
V250/V255 uses the Video template unchanged — that's the brief's call and I'd keep it. A separate wireframe would be identical to 1a/1b/1c with fewer rows, which is exactly the point. The one thing worth watching is that the V250 has genuinely less to say: no storage capacity, four SSDs instead of thirty-six drives, a shorter feature list. Each direction absorbs that differently — Ledger and Rail just get shorter, Banner's fixed-height band and five-up chip strip start to look under-filled. If Banner is the direction you like, the chip strip should be built to take three or five items, not exactly five.
Two calls I made where you said decide for me: the camera matrix leads page 2 on video sheets — it's the number integrators buy on, but it's a table, and tables read better on the technical page than under a hero. And the PDF drives layout; the portal page reflows from the same tokens, so there's one source of truth and the print sheet doesn't inherit screen habits.
